Build a complete, polished, playable browser clone of Age of Empires II. Capture the experience of growing a medieval settlement, developing its economy, advancing through ages, building an army and defeating a computer-controlled rival. Make the implementation decisions yourself. Prioritize an enjoyable game, strong visual presentation and systems that work together. Test it yourself before finishing, and leave it running so I can play it.

Additional requirements:

1. Graphics and visual presentation
Use stylized isometric graphics with rich detail and convincing animation. Make the art cohesive across buildings, terrain, animals, units, water and interface, with readable silhouettes and player colors at normal gameplay zoom. Judge the actual in-game result, not just the title screen. Do not substitute a first-person viewpoint or static concept art for a playable game. No reference images are supplied for this run; create the visual design from this description.

2. Sheep and wildlife
Start with sheep near the player's settlement. Sheep are actual selectable, movable animals: discovering nearby neutral sheep gives that player ownership and control. Enemy units approaching an unguarded sheep can capture it and guide it away. Make ownership clear through selection and team feedback. Specify a consistent proximity/guarding rule so contested sheep do not flicker between owners every frame. Villagers can slaughter sheep and gather their food. Include roaming wildlife such as deer, boar and wolves: huntable animals provide food, prey respond to danger, and predators can threaten exposed units. Animals must interact with the economy and movement systems, rather than being decoration.

3. Water, docks and naval gameplay
Include maps with substantial navigable water, shorelines, islands or connected coastlines, and visible fish resources. Water must matter to gameplay. Villagers can build Docks on valid shoreline sites. Docks produce Fishing Ships, Transport Ships and military ships, including a galley upgrade line, Fire Ships and Demolition Ships, with appropriate age and research requirements. Fishing Ships gather fish and return food to a Dock. Transport Ships load land units, cross water and unload on a valid shore. Warships acquire and fight naval targets, with suitable attacks against coastal targets. Land units cannot walk across deep water and ships cannot sail on land. Give ships visible movement, attack and destruction effects. Computer players must be able to fish, build fleets and use transports when the map requires them; island games must not deadlock because the AI cannot reach its opponent.

4. Responsive unit speech, music and sound
Units should speak short audible acknowledgements when selected and when ordered to move, gather, build or attack. Use varied non-English, civilization-appropriate historical-language-style voice lines, recreating the responsive feeling of selecting and commanding units. These must sound like speech, not generic beeps. Match acknowledgements to the action. Repeated clicks should trigger varied responses without an uncontrolled pile-up of overlapping audio. Do not assume every civilization speaks ancient Greek. Include medieval background game music, environmental sounds, distinct work and combat effects, and separate music, effects and voice volume controls with mute. Activate audio through a user interaction so browser autoplay rules do not leave the game silent. Use locally available or freely usable assets or local synthesis; no paid generation or external paid services. If convincing voices cannot be produced with the available tools, report that limitation explicitly.

5. Full game setup
Let me choose my name, player color, civilization, map type, map size, number of computer opponents, difficulty, teams/free-for-all, starting age, starting resources, visibility/fog of war, game speed and map seed. Include an allow-cheats setting and newcomer tips. Support one human with at least one, two or three computer rivals. Each player needs a distinct color, settlement and functioning economy. Offer land, mixed land/water and island map choices. Every setting must affect the match; do not implement decorative selectors. Show relevant civilization bonuses and available unique units when choosing a civilization.

6. Create-your-own-map editor
Provide a Map Editor accessible from the main menu. Begin with a generated or preset map that I can modify, and also offer a blank map. Let me paint terrain and water and place or erase trees, shrubs, berries, gold, stone, fish, animals, buildings and units. A palette should show the object I am placing. Let me choose the owner for placed units and buildings, add or move Town Centers, define player starting locations, and add multiple computer-controlled players while keeping one human player. Include brush size, selection, move/delete and undo/redo. Save and reload custom maps, and export/import them as files. Let me launch a match using the edited map and return to the editor without losing the saved design. Validate missing starts, overlapping placements and fish/ship placement on land before launching. Custom maps must be playable, not merely drawable.

7. A developed economy, army and technology tree
Build out the familiar progression through Dark, Feudal, Castle and Imperial Ages. Include Town Centers, Houses, Mills, Lumber Camps, Mining Camps, Farms, Barracks, Archery Ranges, Stables, Blacksmiths, Markets, Monasteries, Universities, Siege Workshops, Castles, Towers, Walls, Gates and Docks, plus civilization-specific units and meaningful technologies. Include infantry, spear units, archers, skirmishers, scouts, cavalry, monks and siege engines with distinguishable roles, counters and upgrades. Cavalry must have recognizable mounted units and animations. Age advancement should visibly develop the settlement and unlock the right units and buildings. Use image-based command buttons with hotkey labels separate economic and military construction menus, and tooltips showing names, resource costs, prerequisites and descriptions. Locked actions should visibly explain what is missing. Buildings must produce their intended units or technologies, and resources, population limits, queues and prerequisites must all be enforced.

8. Clearly animated work and combat
Workers visibly perform their actual assigned task: axes swing into trees, pickaxes strike gold and stone, builders hammer, farmers tend crops, and hunters attack animals. Use task-appropriate tools, poses, timing and sound. Animate walking, carrying resources, working, fighting and dying. Resource deposits deplete, felled trees change appearance, and buildings have visible construction progress and damage. Do not represent every activity using the same idle bobbing animation. Keep these actions recognizable at the normal gameplay zoom.

9. Enter-to-type cheat codes
Press Enter to open an in-game chat/cheat input, type a code and press Enter to execute it. While typing, letters must not trigger gameplay shortcuts. Support case-insensitive codes, clear feedback and repeated use when cheats are enabled. For this build, use the following explicit behavior:
- cheese steak jimmy's: add 10,000 food.
- lumberjack: add 10,000 wood.
- robin hood: add 10,000 gold.
- rock on: add 10,000 stone.
- marco: reveal the map.
- polo: remove fog of war.
- aegis: toggle instant construction, training and research.
- how do you turn this on: spawn a controllable Cobra Car at a safe location near my Town Center. It must drive, fire rapidly and deal actual combat damage; it cannot be just a decorative model.
The resource amounts above deliberately follow the Definitive Edition-style values. They add resources, rather than making resources literally infinite. Include the supported codes in the help screen. The remembered BIGDADDY rocket car belongs to Age of Empires I; do not mistakenly label it as the AoE II Cobra cheat.

10. Garrisoning, Town Bell, repairs and healing
Let villagers and appropriate military units garrison in Town Centers, Towers and Castles, with visible capacity, occupants and ungarrison controls. Garrisoned units must leave the map safely, contribute appropriately to defensive fire, and reappear at a valid exit when released. The Town Bell recalls villagers to nearby available shelter; switching it off returns surviving villagers to their previous work. Avoid villagers remaining trapped indefinitely or the AI repeatedly stopping its whole economy over a minor threat. Villagers repair damaged buildings, siege engines and ships using appropriate resources, with visible progress and repair animations. Monks heal damaged friendly living units; distinguish healing from repairing machines and buildings.

11. Monastery / church, monks and relics
Include a recognizable monastery/church building unlocked at the appropriate age. It trains monks and researches relevant technologies. Monks can heal friendly units, attempt enemy conversions with a visible delay and cooldown, and collect relics. Depositing relics in a Monastery produces periodic gold; show the stored relic count and income. Make retrieval/drop behavior reliable when a monk dies or a Monastery is destroyed. The AI should use these systems where appropriate.

12. Diplomacy and trade
Provide a diplomacy panel for matches with multiple computer players, showing each player's name, color and current relationship. Allow alliance and peace proposals and declarations of hostility, with clear acceptance/rejection and consequences. AI players should respond consistently rather than automatically accepting everything. Allied players must stop targeting each other; declaring war restores hostile targeting. Let players send resource tribute with any fee clearly shown. Markets train Trade Carts that travel between friendly players' Markets and return gold; Docks can train Trade Cogs for equivalent sea trade between friendly Docks. Trade requires an actual valid route and surviving units, with income related to travel distance. Respond sensibly when a destination is destroyed or diplomacy changes. Explain the win condition when alliances exist so matches cannot become unwinnable after making peace.

13. Elevation and terrain in combat
Include clearly visible hills and height differences, not just flat terrain painted different colors. Higher ground provides an explicit, consistent combat advantage and lower ground a corresponding disadvantage; document the rule in help/tooltips. Terrain and obstacles must affect navigation and positioning: forests, cliffs, shorelines and water restrict movement appropriately, with valid routes for land units and ships. Ranged attacks, targeting and pathfinding must respect the intended elevation and obstacle rules. Extend the editor with elevation painting so custom maps use the same rules. Ensure generated maps and AI navigation can handle hills and narrow passages without stranding players.

14. Completion and self-testing
Deliver working connected systems, not placeholders or controls that do nothing. Test a complete economy-to-combat progression; sheep capture and recapture; animal hunting; a fishing economy; naval combat and transport landing; a match against multiple opponents; editor save/reload and play; unit acknowledgements and volume controls; cheat entry; garrison/ungarrison and Town Bell work resumption; repairs, healing and relic income; diplomacy changes and functioning trade routes; uphill/downhill combat; and game restart. Check the interface at typical desktop viewport sizes and inspect the visual result for rich detail, readable isometric graphics and convincing animation. Report which tests actually passed, any untested behavior and any remaining omissions. Preserve editable source and leave a playable local preview running.
